# Mocking up before building

## In short

Words describe a screen badly. Show what will be built, get a yes, and then build exactly that.

## Use this when

- You are about to build or change a screen someone will look at.
- The request describes the screen in words only.
- There is more than one sensible way to lay it out.

A change nobody will see, such as a speed fix, does not need a mockup.

## Do this

1. **Draw before you build.** Use the cheapest form that answers the question: a rough sketch for where things go, a clickable page for how the steps flow.
2. **Fill it with realistic content.** Use believable names, long values, large numbers and awkward cases. Filler text hides the problems a mockup exists to find.
3. **Show every state**: nothing there yet, loading, full, and something gone wrong. Show what follows each button.
4. **Show the whole journey** a person takes, from arriving to finishing.
5. **Where the choice is open, show two or three versions side by side**, and say which you would pick and why.
6. **Say what the mockup leaves out**, and mark anything in it that is not real.
7. **Get the owner's approval before building.** Their yes covers what they were shown and nothing else. Write down what was approved and what they asked to change, using `mockup-approval-template.md`.
8. **Build what was approved, then compare.** Put the finished screen beside the mockup. Any difference goes back to the owner.

## Warning signs

- Building started from a written description alone.
- A mockup with one tidy example and no empty or error state.
- "The owner liked the idea" treated as approval of a screen they never saw.
- A finished screen that differs from the mockup, with nobody told.

## Why it matters

A mockup takes minutes to change. A built screen takes hours. Most disagreements about a screen surface the first time someone sees it, so make that moment come before the building.
